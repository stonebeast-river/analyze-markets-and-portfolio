# Transmission map

Treat every relationship below as a hypothesis to test, not an automatic rule. The same shock can produce different outcomes depending on expectations, positioning, valuation, and policy reaction.

## Macro drivers

| Driver | Primary channels to inspect | Commonly affected areas | Main ambiguity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Growth surprise | earnings revisions, credit demand, cyclicality, risk appetite | broad equities, cyclicals, commodities, credit | stronger growth can also raise yields and compress long-duration valuations |
| Inflation surprise | real income, margins, rate expectations, discount rates | bonds, growth stocks, banks, commodities, gold | supply-led and demand-led inflation transmit differently |
| Central-bank repricing | policy path, real yields, liquidity, currency | duration assets, banks, FX, gold | easing caused by recession can initially hurt risk assets |
| Fiscal or credit impulse | demand, infrastructure, leverage, currency confidence | China equities, cyclicals, commodities, rates | implementation and private-sector response may lag headlines |
| Dollar move | global liquidity, translation, commodity pricing, capital flows | emerging markets, QDII NAV, gold, commodities | dollar strength may hedge a foreign holding in renminbi terms |
| Geopolitical shock | safe havens, energy supply, trade routes, risk premium | gold, oil, defense, airlines, broad risk assets | first-day safe-haven moves can reverse when escalation is contained |
| Earnings revisions | cash-flow expectations, valuation support, sector leadership | equity indices and sectors | index gains concentrated in a few firms may not indicate broad health |
| Positioning and flows | forced buying or selling, volatility, correlation | all liquid assets | flows can explain price without changing fundamentals |

## Asset-specific checks

### Mainland China broad equities

Inspect domestic growth expectations, policy implementation, credit impulse, property and consumption, corporate earnings, valuation, northbound or domestic institutional flows when reliable, and renminbi conditions. Separate policy announcement from funded implementation and economic transmission.

### Hong Kong equities

Combine mainland fundamentals with offshore liquidity, dollar rates, risk premium, index composition, and southbound flows. Do not treat Hong Kong as a simple duplicate of A-shares.

### US growth equities and Nasdaq-linked exposure

Inspect earnings revisions and breadth alongside real yields and valuation duration. A higher yield is not automatically bearish if it reflects stronger earnings growth. For a renminbi investor, separate underlying-index return from USD/CNY translation, fund tracking difference, fees, and QDII valuation lag.

### Gold

Inspect real yields, dollar direction, central-bank demand, geopolitical risk, inflation credibility, futures positioning, and physical-market conditions. For a domestic gold fund, distinguish:

1. international gold price;
2. USD/CNY translation;
3. domestic exchange premium or discount;
4. fund tracking difference and fees.

Do not use nominal interest rates alone to explain gold.

### Bonds and cash

Distinguish policy rates, nominal yields, real yields, curve shape, credit spreads, and duration. Falling yields can signal helpful easing or harmful growth stress.

## Fund-product frictions

- Confirm the exact share class and its fee structure.
- Account for subscription, redemption, and valuation cutoffs.
- Do not present intraday estimates as confirmed fund NAV.
- For QDII products, identify different trading days and delayed overseas-market information.
- Compare the fund with the correct benchmark and currency basis.
- Treat tracking error, tracking difference, cash drag, limits, and premium or discount as product effects distinct from the underlying asset thesis.
